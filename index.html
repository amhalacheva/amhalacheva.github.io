Research

Towards embodied scene intelligence: enabling machines to understand, describe, and reason about the physical world.

My work investigates how intelligent systems can obtain an understanding of real-world 3D environments. While modern AI can recognize objects and answer questions about images, embodied agents require a deeper understanding: they must reason about physical structure, semantic meaning, and spatial relationships simultaneously.

To address this challenge, I explore scene-centric 3D methods that connect perception, language, and interaction. My research spans articulated object understanding, 3D vision-language models, and robust grounding mechanisms that encourage genuine visual-spatial reasoning rather than shortcut learning.

I

Physical Understanding

Understanding how objects are structured, articulated, and capable of interaction within real-world environments.

II

Semantic Understanding

Understanding what is present in a scene and connecting visual observations with open-ended language and world knowledge.

III

Spatial Reasoning

Understanding where things are, how they relate to one another, and reasoning about locations, context, and geometry in 3D space.
